\section{Introduction}
% Drafted from SPEC.md section 1. The introduction is the one section that has to be in the
% first author's voice; treat every paragraph below as a target to rewrite, not text to keep.

Trajectory optimization among obstacles is a nonconvex problem, and every practical method
convexifies it somewhere. The family this paper belongs to convexifies by building, before the
solve, a collection of convex regions of free space and a combinatorial structure that selects
among them: safe corridors, and more recently graphs of convex sets over which a single convex
relaxation is solved~\cite{marcucci2024gcs,marcucci2023science}. Lifting time into the state
turns a moving obstacle into a static set in one higher dimension, and the same machinery
extends to obstacles in motion~\cite{osburn2025stgcs,tang2025stgcs}. The cost of that route is
the construction of the regions themselves. Free space has to be represented, sampled and carved
into convex pieces; the number of pieces grows with the geometry of the hazards and with the
dimension of the lifted space, and the resulting graph is what the optimizer searches.

This paper asks what the optimizer actually needs from a hazard, and answers with an interface
rather than a representation. For each segment of a B\'ezier trajectory and each hazard, the
construction below asks the keep-out set for two things: the point of the set nearest the
segment, and a rigorous bound on how far the set reaches in one direction. From those two answers
it returns one supporting half-space, imposed on the segment's control points, and by the convex
hull property of B\'ezier curves that half-space is satisfied along the whole segment, for all
times in it. The half-spaces are rebuilt around the current iterate at every step of a
successive-convexification loop, so the convex subproblem is a quadratic program with second-order
cones whose size is linear in the number of segments times the number of hazards, with no integer
variables, no region graph, and no cell selection. Nothing about the hazard's shape enters except
through the two oracle answers: a set and its convex hull have identical support in every
direction, so the keep-out set need not be convex, connected, or even representable.

The evidence that this is an interface and not a retrospective description is a hazard that
nobody would choose to carve. A body moving on a curved path above a ground station casts a
shadow that twists, widens with distance from the station, and is not convex in space-time. The
same construction and the same row builder produce the walls for a static ball, for the lifted
tube of a moving body, and for that shadow; there is no occlusion-specific branch anywhere, and
with no station present every formula reduces to the plain tube identically. Keeping a
line of sight through a moving occluder costs one paragraph of this paper because the oracle does
not care what shape the hazard is.

Two things follow from building constraints from a local oracle rather than from a global
structure, and the paper states both plainly. The first is what is owed to the reader: a
construction that sees the hazard only through a ball around the current segment must say what it
covers. Section~\ref{sec:coverage} gives a condition on the clipping radius under which the
segment certificate covers the whole keep-out set, names the regime in which it does not, and
counts that regime in every reported run. Methods in this family report that the solver
converged; none, to our knowledge, report coverage. The second is the price: the method is local,
and the passing side of each hazard is inherited from the initialization. That is a design
position, and its consequences are measured rather than conceded.

\paragraph{Contributions.} One claim, with three consequences that are evidence it buys
something, and one guarantee that was not given up.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The claim.} Continuous-time avoidance constraints can be built from a support
  oracle on the hazard (Section~\ref{sec:oracle}): a nearest point and a rigorous support bound,
  per segment and per hazard, yield one supporting half-space of a clipped piece of the keep-out
  set, imposed on the segment's control points and rebuilt per iterate.
\item \textbf{Hazard shape is free.} A body of arbitrary polynomial motion, a finite lifetime, or
  a twisting shadow produces the same rows as a static ball (Section~\ref{sec:instantiations}).
\item \textbf{Timing is a decision inside the same convex subproblem.} Arrival time, the speed
  cap and the time penalty are variables and rows of one QP; there is no path-then-time-allocation
  stage (Section~\ref{sec:subproblem}).
\item \textbf{Cost is independent of hazard geometry.} Rows are linear in segments times
  hazards whatever the shape; answering the oracle is local work at one point
  (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\item \textbf{Coverage is stated and counted} (Section~\ref{sec:coverage}), and every reported
  guarantee is re-checked by a certificate that shares nothing with the construction it checks.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
% Only claims read from the source are asserted. The four references marked \todo were read

\paragraph{Configuration-time space.} Planning among moving obstacles in a space whose last
coordinate is time goes back to Erdmann and Lozano-P\'erez~\cite{erdmann1987}, who represented
space-time approximately by two-dimensional slices. Search-based methods discretize it
directly (safe-interval path planning~\cite{phillips2011sipp}); sampling-based methods sample
$(x,t)$ with a free arrival time~\cite{grothe2022strrt}. Both produce discrete or sampled
trajectories rather than a smooth curve with a continuous-time guarantee.

\paragraph{Corridors and graphs of convex sets.} The convex-optimization branch of the family
builds convex sets in the planning space before the solve and constrains the curve inside them.
Semantic corridors~\cite{ding2019ssc} do so along a reference; graphs of convex
sets~\cite{marcucci2024gcs,marcucci2023science} replace the corridor with a graph of regions and
a single convex relaxation over it, with the regions generated by iterative regional inflation
(IRIS)~\cite{deits2015iris}. \todo{Verify from source: GCS (arXiv 2205.04422 and the SIAM paper)
and IRIS were read via summariser and abstract only.} Tang et al.~\cite{tang2025stgcs} extend
the graph to space-time for multi-robot planning, optimizing arrival time inside it, with
piecewise-constant-velocity trajectories and other robots' planned paths as the only moving
obstacles. Osburn, Peterson and Salmon~\cite{osburn2025stgcs} are the nearest prior work to this
paper: B\'ezier control points carrying an explicit time component, IRIS-sampled convex sets in
two spatial dimensions plus time, static or constant-velocity prismatic obstacles, and Clarabel as
the conic solver. In their formulation the end point is pinned at a fixed point of space-time and
the cost is spatial path length, so timing is not optimized; their own Table~I shows set count and
solve time rising with the number of IRIS samples while coverage of free space remains incomplete,
which is the observation this paper starts from. \todo{SPOT (arXiv 2602.01189), the only
member of the family in three spatial dimensions plus time, caps its horizon at two seconds with
binned time --- read via summariser only; verify before citing.}

\paragraph{Supporting planes and the hull property.} The continuous-time guarantee below is the
convex hull property of B\'ezier curves, standard machinery used by B\'ezier and B-spline planners
for years; it is cited, not claimed. Zhang et al.\ give a sufficient condition under which the hull
property holds in general spatio-temporal corridors~\cite{zhang2021hull}
\todo{verify from source}. MADER~\cite{tordesillas2022mader} treats the separating plane as a
decision variable, the alternative to linearizing it as done here.

\paragraph{What is not attempted.} A coordinate transform that convexifies free space cannot
exist: a convex set is simply connected and free space around an obstacle is not, and
homeomorphisms preserve that. The strongest published version, navigation
functions~\cite{rimon1991}, deforms geometry and leaves topology alone, which is why its guarantee
holds from almost all initial conditions. \todo{Rimon and Koditschek read at abstract level only.}

\paragraph{The strengths of the graph family, stated here and in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.}
Graph-of-convex-sets methods need no initial guess; they are globally optimal with respect to
their graph; and their small cases solve in a fraction of a second. This paper is local, seeded,
and argues about how cost scales with hazard geometry and dimension, not about wall-clock on
small problems.

\section{The lifted setting}
\label{sec:setting}

\begin{definition}[Lifted space]
\label{def:lift}
Let $\ds\in\{2,3\}$ be the spatial dimension. The lifted space is $\R^{\ds+1}$ with points
$z=(x,\tau)$, $x\in\R^{\ds}$, $\tau\in\R$. The axis scale is one: $\|\cdot\|$ is the Euclidean
norm on $\R^{\ds+1}$ and every ball, distance and projection below is taken in it. Nothing in the
construction singles out the time axis.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Trajectory]
\label{def:curve}
The decision variable is the control polygon $P_0,\dots,P_N\in\R^{\ds+1}$, $P_j=(x_j,t_j)$,
stacked into $\mathbf p\in\R^{(N+1)(\ds+1)}$. With $u\in[0,1]$ the curve parameter (not time),
\[
Z(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}B_j^N(u)\,P_j,\qquad B_j^N(u)=\binom{N}{j}u^j(1-u)^{N-j},\qquad
\tau(u)=\sum_j B_j^N(u)\,t_j .
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Segments]
\label{def:segments}
De Casteljau subdivision into $M$ segments gives row-stochastic matrices $A^{(k)}$ and, for
segment $k$, control points, centroid and radius
\[
Q^{(k)}_i=\sum_j A^{(k)}_{ij}P_j,\qquad
c^{(k)}=\tfrac{1}{N+1}\sum_i Q^{(k)}_i=\sum_j w^{(k)}_j P_j,\qquad
E^{(k)}=\max_i\|Q^{(k)}_i-c^{(k)}\|,
\]
with $w^{(k)}\ge 0$, $\sum_j w^{(k)}_j=1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Hull property]
\label{lem:hull}
For $u$ in segment $k$'s parameter interval, $Z(u)\in\conv\{Q^{(k)}_i\}_{i=0}^{N}$.
\end{lemma}
Standard; see e.g.~\cite{zhang2021hull} for its use in corridors.

\begin{definition}[Keep-out set of a moving body]
\label{def:koz}
Body $m$ has known motion $\pi_m$ on its existence window $[T^0_m,T^1_m]$ and radius $r_m$. Its
lifted centreline and keep-out set are
\[
\gamma_m(\tau)=(\pi_m(\tau),\tau),\qquad
\Gamma_m=\gamma_m([T^0_m,T^1_m]),\qquad
\Kz_m=\Gamma_m\oplus\ball(0,r_m).
\]
If $\pi_m$ is polynomial of degree $N_m$, then $\gamma_m$ is a B\'ezier curve in $\R^{\ds+1}$
whose first and last control points carry the existence window. $\Kz_m$ is not convex unless
$\pi_m$ is affine.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The last sentence is why the rest of the paper exists: a nonconvex keep-out set admits no single
supporting half-space, and the hull property alone certifies nothing against it.
\end{remark}

\section{What the construction requires: a support oracle}
\label{sec:oracle}

Fix a segment with centroid $c$ and a clipping ball $\ball(c,\rclip)$, defined in
Section~\ref{sec:construction}. The construction asks the keep-out set $\Kz$ for the following
and nothing more.
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{O1}] The nearest point of $\Kz$ to $c$, or equivalently the direction from $\Kz$
  back to $c$. (Used for the wall's normal.)
\item[\textbf{O2}] A rigorous upper bound on $\max\{\,n^\top z : z\in\Kz\cap\ball(c,\rclip)\,\}$
  for a given direction $n$. (Used for the wall's offset.)
\item[\textbf{O3}] A partition of $\Kz\cap\ball(c,\rclip)$ into pieces, one wall each. Any
  partition is sound; the choice affects quality only.
\item[\textbf{O4}] Optionally, the active face of $\Kz$ at the nearest point, for the rotation
  term of the linearized row (Section~\ref{sec:subproblem}). Without it the rows remain sound
  and converge more slowly.
\end{itemize}

\begin{lemma}[Support functions do not see convexity]
\label{lem:support}
For a compact set $S\subset\R^{d}$ and any direction $n$,
$h_S(n):=\max_{z\in S}n^\top z=\max_{z\in\conv S}n^\top z=h_{\conv S}(n)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A linear functional attains its maximum over a compact convex set at an extreme point, and every
extreme point of $\conv S$ belongs to $S$.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:support} is what makes O2 answerable without ever forming a convex
over-approximation of the hazard: the wall built against a piece of $\Kz$ and the wall built
against that piece's convex hull are the same wall, so the convexity of $\Kz$ is irrelevant to the
construction. It is also the step at which a tangent plane fails: a tangent to the tube at the
nearest centreline point is a support of $\Kz$ only when $\Kz$ is convex.

\section{Instantiations: ball, curved tube, shadow}
\label{sec:instantiations}

\paragraph{Ball and lifted tube.} A static ball is Definition~\ref{def:koz} with $\pi_m$ constant;
a body of polynomial motion is the same definition with a B\'ezier centreline. Neither needs
anything beyond Section~\ref{sec:oracle}.

\paragraph{Shadow.} A ground station is a point $p\in\R^{\ds}$ present at every instant.

\begin{definition}[Center surface]
\label{def:center-surface}
Stretching the centreline of body $m$ away from the station,
\[
\Sigma_m(\tau,u)=\bigl(p+u\,(\pi_m(\tau)-p),\ \tau\bigr),\quad u\ge 1,\qquad
\Kz_m^{p}=\bigcup_{\tau,\,u\ge 1}\ball\bigl(\Sigma_m(\tau,u),\ u\,r_m\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}
At $u=1$ this is the body itself with its own radius; what widens with $u$ is the shadow, because a
point source at finite distance casts an umbra of constant angular thickness. Time is not
stretched: the station maps each instant to itself, so every shadow wall carries a nonzero time
component like every other wall.

\begin{lemma}[Shadow of a convex body at one instant]
\label{lem:shadow}
For a point observer and a convex body not containing it, the set of positions whose line of
sight to the observer meets the body is convex.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A shadow position is a body point scaled from the observer by a factor at least one. A convex
combination of two such positions is a convex combination of body points scaled by the averaged
factor, which is at least one, and a convex combination of body points is a body point.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The sweep is the umbra]
\label{lem:umbra}
Position $q$ is occluded at instant $\tau$ if and only if $q=p+u(z-p)$ for some body point $z$
and some $u\ge 1$, if and only if $(q,\tau)\in\Kz^p_m$. With no station, $u\equiv 1$ and
$\Kz^p_m=\Kz_m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}
The shadow of a \emph{moving} body in space-time is not convex --- the cone rotates as the body
crosses the sight line --- which is exactly why the wall below is a support of a clipped piece and
not a tangent plane, for the shadow as for the tube. For fixed $u$ the stretch is affine, so
$\Sigma_m(\cdot,u)$ is again a B\'ezier curve and every bound of Section~\ref{sec:construction}
applies to it unchanged. There is no occlusion-specific branch in the construction; the body and
its shadow are one keep-out set read at two stretch factors.
\end{remark}

\section{The clip, the wall, and the segment certificate}
\label{sec:construction}
Everything in this section is per segment $k$ and per body $m$, with the centroid $c=c^{(k)}$
fixed at the reference iterate.

\begin{definition}[Nearest point and clipping radius]
\label{def:clip}
\[
\tau^\star\in\arg\min_{\tau\in[T^0_m,T^1_m]}\|c-\gamma_m(\tau)\|,\qquad
f=\gamma_m(\tau^\star),\qquad d=\|c-f\|,
\]
\[
\rclip=\max\bigl(\min(d,\,r_{\mathrm{clip,max}}),\ r_{\mathrm{floor}}\bigr),\qquad
r_{\mathrm{clip,max}}=r_m+E^{(k)}+\Delta\sqrt{\ds+1},
\]
where $\Delta$ is the trust radius of the current iteration and the floor is either the
construction floor $r_m$ or the reach floor $\max(r_m,\,E^{(k)}+\Delta\sqrt{\ds+1})$ of
Section~\ref{sec:coverage}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Clipped keep-out volume and approaches]
\label{def:clipped}
$\Lz^{(k)}_m=\Kz_m\cap\ball(c,\rclip)$. Let
$I=\{\tau:\|\gamma_m(\tau)-c\|\le\rclip+r_m\}$, cut $I$ at every interior local maximum of
$\tau\mapsto\|\gamma_m(\tau)-c\|$, and call each resulting sub-interval $I_\ell$ an
\emph{approach}, with piece
$\Lz^{(k)}_{m,\ell}=(\gamma_m(I_\ell)\oplus\ball(0,r_m))\cap\ball(c,\rclip)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Band containment]
\label{lem:band}
$\Lz^{(k)}_m\subseteq\gamma_m(I)\oplus\ball(0,r_m)$, and the pieces cover $\Lz^{(k)}_m$ for any
partition of $I$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A point of $\Lz^{(k)}_m$ is within $r_m$ of some $\gamma_m(\tau)$ and within $\rclip$ of $c$; the
triangle inequality puts $\tau$ in $I$.
\end{proof}

One wall per approach, never one per body: two separated lumps inside the ball are two
approaches, and a single lump that wraps the centroid is also two, because its single wall is
non-separating. The cut position is a quality choice; soundness holds for any partition (O3).

\begin{definition}[Supporting half-space]
\label{def:wall}
For one piece $\Lz=\Lz^{(k)}_{m,\ell}$,
\[
y^\star\in\arg\min_{y\in\Lz}\|c-y\|,\qquad
n=\frac{c-y^\star}{\|c-y^\star\|},\qquad
b=h_{\Lz}(n)=\max_{z\in\Lz}n^\top z .
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Half-space containment]
\label{lem:containment}
$\Lz\subseteq\{z:n^\top z\le b\}$, with no convexity assumption on $\Kz_m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[The direction does not depend on $y^\star$]
\label{lem:direction}
With $f_\ell=\gamma_m(\tau^\star_\ell)$ the nearest centreline point within approach $\ell$,
$n=(c-f_\ell)/\|c-f_\ell\|$ whenever the quotient of Definition~\ref{def:wall} exists, and this
form is used also when it does not (centroid inside the keep-out set). The only undefined case is
$c=f_\ell$; it, and a station lying within $r_m$ of the centreline, are counted as dropped walls,
never silently treated as out of reach.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Computing $b$.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:hull} applied to the body's own B\'ezier centreline,
subdivided over $I_\ell$ (and over the stretch parameter $u$ when a station is present), the
support of the piece is at most the largest $n^\top$ of the subdivided control points plus $r_m$.
The construction uses this rigorous ceiling $\hat b\ge b$; Lemma~\ref{lem:containment} holds a
fortiori. Hulling sampled centreline points is never done, because a curve bulges outside such a
hull.

\begin{definition}[Exact rows]
\label{def:rows}
For every control point of segment $k$ and every wall $(k,m,\ell)$:
$\;n_{km\ell}^\top Q^{(k)}_i\ \ge\ \hat b_{km\ell}$, $i=0,\dots,N$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Segment certificate]
\label{prop:certificate}
If the $N+1$ rows of one wall hold, then $n^\top Z(u)\ge\hat b$ for every $u$ in segment $k$, hence
$Z(u)\notin\operatorname{int}\Lz$: the curve does not enter the interior of the clipped keep-out
volume for all times in the segment.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:hull} writes $Z(u)$ as a convex combination of the $Q^{(k)}_i$; linearity gives
$n^\top Z(u)\ge\hat b$; an interior point of $\Lz$ has $n^\top z<b\le\hat b$.
\end{proof}
The proof uses the same $n$ for all $i$; one plane per control point would destroy it.

\section{Coverage}
\label{sec:coverage}
Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate} certifies against $\Kz_m\cap\ball(c,\rclip)$, not against
$\Kz_m$. The trust region closes the difference, and nothing else does.

\begin{lemma}[Reachability]
\label{lem:reach}
If $\|\mathbf p^{+}-\mathbf p\|_\infty\le\Delta$, then for every $u$ in segment $k$,
$Z^{+}(u)\in\ball\bigl(c,\;E^{(k)}+\Delta\sqrt{\ds+1}\bigr)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each $Q^{+}_i-Q_i$ is a convex combination of vectors of $\infty$-norm at most $\Delta$, so
$\|Q^{+}_i-c\|\le E^{(k)}+\Delta\sqrt{\ds+1}$, and the ball is convex.
\end{proof}

\begin{condition}[Coverage]
\label{cond:coverage}
$\rclip\ \ge\ E^{(k)}+\Delta\sqrt{\ds+1}$.
\end{condition}
Under Condition~\ref{cond:coverage} the clipping ball contains the whole next-iterate segment, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate} certifies against the full $\Kz_m$. The condition does not
involve $r_m$ or the hazard. With the construction floor $r_m$ it fails exactly when the segment is
close --- $r_m<d<E^{(k)}+\Delta\sqrt{\ds+1}$ --- which is where the constraint binds; this is the
hole. The reach floor makes the condition hold unconditionally, at the price of conservatism where
the row is active, paid in iterations rather than correctness. Whichever floor a run uses, the
number of walls failing the condition is exported per iteration, and no figure in this paper is
drawn from a run in which that count is nonzero.

\section{The convex subproblem and the algorithm}
\label{sec:subproblem}

\paragraph{Subproblem at reference $\mathbf p^{\mathrm{ref}}$.}
\[
\min_{\mathbf p,\ \sigma\ge 0}\quad
\tfrac12\,\mathbf p^\top H\mathbf p\;+\;w_t\,t_N\;+\;\lambda\sum\sigma
\]
subject to: boundary conditions ($P_0$ fixed, $x_N$ fixed, $t_N$ free or pinned); a workspace
box; monotonicity $t_{j+1}-t_j\ge\delta>0$; the slant limit
$\|x_{j+1}-x_j\|\le v_{\max}(t_{j+1}-t_j)$ as a second-order cone with no slack; the linearized
keep-out and line-of-sight rows with elastic slack $\sigma$; and the trust region
$\|\mathbf p-\mathbf p^{\mathrm{ref}}\|_\infty\le\Delta$.

\paragraph{The linearized row, and what it does not guarantee.} The exact row of
Definition~\ref{def:rows} freezes $n$ and $\hat b$ at the reference, but both move with the
centroid once the control points move. The row imposed in the subproblem is the first-order model
\[
g_{ki}(\mathbf p^{\mathrm{ref}})+\sum_j\bigl(A^{(k)}_{ij}\,n+w^{(k)}_j\,\kappa_{ki}\bigr)^{\!\top}
(P_j-P_j^{\mathrm{ref}})\ \ge\ 0,\qquad
\kappa_{ki}=\frac{(I-P_F)(I-nn^\top)\,(Q^{(k)}_i-s)}{\|c-y^\star\|},
\]
with $s$ the support point on the plane and $P_F$ the projector onto the active face at $y^\star$
(O4). \textbf{This row is not conservative}: a point satisfying it may violate the exact row.
Soundness is untouched because every certificate, merit and gate in this paper rebuilds the exact
rows at the iterate it grades; the linearized row only steers the solver.

\paragraph{Objective.} $H$ is assembled from second differences of the spatial coordinates only,
so it is blind to timing; physics enters through the constraints. Arrival time is $t_N$ because a
B\'ezier passes through its last control point, so its penalty is linear and the subproblem stays a
QP with cones. \todo{State the slant-limit sufficiency lemma (clearing the denominator requires
$\tau'>0$, which is what makes monotonicity load-bearing) and the trap: a time penalty with no
speed cap collapses arrival to $\delta N$.}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
In this order, each in its own paragraph: local and seeded, the passing side inherited from the
initialization, stated as the price of a local oracle; ``converged'' is feasibility plus no
further progress, not stationarity; the hole of Section~\ref{sec:coverage} and its measured rate;
