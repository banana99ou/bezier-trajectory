\section{Introduction: the question and the reader}
Can a local trajectory optimizer handle a complicated moving hazard using only
queries to its geometry? We investigate a construction that asks for a nearest
point and a bound on the hazard's extent in a chosen direction, then builds a
separating half-space for each trajectory segment. These queries are the
\emph{support-oracle interface} studied here.

The intended reader develops motion-planning or trajectory-optimization methods.
The motivating example is a moving body's line-of-sight shadow: the trajectory
must avoid collision while retaining visibility to a ground station. The paper
asks whether the same constraint construction can handle both hazards efficiently
and with an explicit continuous-time safety condition.

\textbf{Proposed contribution.} A local supporting-half-space construction for
moving hazards, its coverage condition, and demonstrations for curved obstacle
motion and moving shadows. Speed relative to existing planners remains an
experimental question.

\section{Related work: where the contribution must stand}
Space-time GCS already plans around moving obstacles; Osburn et al.\ use
B\'ezier trajectories, while Tang et al.\ optimize arrival time in a space-time
graph. MADER already uses separating planes for trajectory avoidance.
Neither the space-time representation nor the B\'ezier hull property is new.
The paper must establish what the proposed support-bound construction adds,
including comparison with a conventional local optimizer.

\section{Method: the construction and its guarantee}
Represent a trajectory by a B\'ezier curve $Z(u)=(x(u),t(u))$, where $u$ is a
curve parameter and $t(u)$ increases. Both spatial and temporal control points
are optimized, subject to boundary conditions and a speed cap.

For a segment with control points $Q_i$ and centroid $c$, clip a hazard to a
ball $B(c,r)$. For each relevant piece $\mathcal L$ of this clipped set, choose
a unit normal $n$ pointing from the hazard toward $c$, and compute
\[
 \widehat b\ \ge\ \sup_{z\in\mathcal L}n^\top z,
 \qquad n^\top Q_i\ \ge\ \widehat b+\varepsilon\quad\text{for every }i,
\]
where $\varepsilon>0$ is a separation margin. The hull property then separates
the entire segment from that piece. Applying the construction to every piece
covers the clipped hazard. It covers the full hazard if the clipping ball
contains every point the segment can reach within the trust region.

\textbf{Algorithm and proof obligations.} Rebuild the walls at each iterate,
solve a convex subproblem, and check the candidate using the exact constraints.
The objective combines spatial control-point smoothness, an optional arrival-time
penalty, and elastic penalties. Prove the support bound and coverage condition;
explain how the independent clearance check is computed. Linearized rows alone
are not a safety certificate.

\section{Discussion: what a reviewer will challenge}
\textbf{Novelty.} Which part improves on existing separating-plane methods?
Identify the construction and its useful consequences precisely.

\textbf{Cost.} Fixed approach count and polynomial degree give a row count linear
in segments times hazards. Approach count, oracle evaluation, and iteration count
may still depend on geometry. Total runtime must be measured.

\textbf{Fairness.} Our solver is local and depends on initialization. Graph
methods search route alternatives represented by their graph. Compare quality
and success as well as runtime; unrelated examples do not establish a speedup.

\textbf{Scope.} Known deterministic motion, offline planning, binary occlusion,
and kinematic constraints. No global-optimality or stationarity claim. Safety
claims require the stated coverage and verification conditions.

\section{Conclusion to write after the experiments}
State which hazards the construction handled, under which safety conditions,
and the measured trade-off against the baselines. The journal extends the KSAS
two-page paper with the full construction and proofs, coverage analysis, and
comparative experiments. No performance conclusion is available yet.
